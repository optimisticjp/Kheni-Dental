Six pictures on your website are not your clinic

When the site was built, some photographs were not available yet, so computer-generated stand-ins were used to finish the layouts. They were always meant to be temporary.

Six of them are still there, and they are the ones that matter, because they are captioned as your rooms. The waiting area shown at Yogi Chowk has teal armchairs, a terrazzo table and a tropical plant. It is a nice room. It is not your room.

This is worth fixing before advertising starts, for three plain reasons: a patient who arrives expecting that room and finds a different one starts the visit already unsure; Google Ads can treat a landing page that misrepresents a business as a policy problem; and your actual clinics look good, so there is nothing to gain by hiding them.

The good news

Every one of these slots is already built and sized on the website. Send the photographs and they drop straight in. Nothing needs redesigning, and no page changes shape.

Before you start

How to take them

A phone from the last few years is genuinely fine. These eight habits matter far more than the camera.

Daylight, lights onShoot in the morning or late afternoon with blinds open and the room lights on. Never use the phone flash.
Hold it levelPhone upright, roughly chest height, back straight. Tilting up or down makes rooms look smaller than they are.
Tidy first, then shootClear cups, papers, cables, bins and personal items. Thirty seconds of tidying beats any editing.
Landscape for roomsTurn the phone sideways for rooms and buildings. Keep it upright only for people.
Stand in the cornerFor a room, stand in a corner and shoot across it. You get the whole space rather than one wall.
No filters, no beauty modeSend the original file. Filters and skin smoothing are obvious on a dental website and cost trust.
Send as files, not photosOn WhatsApp choose Document rather than Gallery. Sending as a photo shrinks it badly.
Take three of eachTwo steps left, two steps right. It costs nothing and one is always better than the others.

On patients: nobody needs to appear in any photograph on this list. Where a person helps, a staff member works perfectly. If a patient is ever in a picture, we need their written permission first, and no faces are needed for anything here.

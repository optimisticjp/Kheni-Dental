## Why there are four roses

The logo's own rose (#9B6665) cannot do every job and stay legible:

- as small text on ivory it reaches only 4.35:1
- as a button fill it reaches 4.05:1 with an ink label and 4.35:1 with ivory
- as text on ink it reaches 4.05:1

So the brand rose is kept for marks, fills and shapes, where contrast rules
ask for 3:1 rather than 4.5:1, and three contrast-solved siblings carry the
work it cannot:

| Token | Value | Job |
|---|---|---|
| `--brand-rose` | #9B6665 | the logo mark, fills, decorative shapes |
| `--brand-rose-strong` | #B87872 | primary button fill, ink label at 5.39:1 |
| `--brand-rose-text` | #895A59 | links, labels and the highlighted word on light |
| `--brand-rose-on-dark` | #B88C8A | the same jobs on ink |

Champagne needed the same treatment. #D3B985 is 9.97:1 on ink and perfect for
stars, but only 3.69:1 as small text on ivory, so light surfaces use the
deeper #7B6235 instead.
